## 3. Rationale for the Change
* **Dev Miss Analysis:** As documented in `evidence/exp_baseline_dev_miss_reasons.txt`, the baseline prompt missed 3 dev postings (`5438044008`, `4631822008`, and `5198255008`). All three misses shared a common thread: Claude cited implicit level signals (such as high salary bands and demanding role scopes) to reject them despite the lack of explicit years or PhD prerequisites.
* **Alternative Rejections:** Other candidates from `REVISION_CANDIDATES.md` were rejected because the dev miss logs provided no evidence supporting them. (Note: While exploring options, I accidentally applied both candidate changes to the revision file; diff caught it before any run, and I reset it to the single change).

## 5. What Can Be Concluded
* **Dev Split Improvement:** On the 10 dev postings, the revision prompt successfully fixed all 3 previous misses, bringing accuracy from 70% to 100% without breaking any of the 7 previously correct verdicts. This performance gain is consistent with the change removing false rejections driven by salary ranges and implied scope, though with one call per posting, some of the gain may be noise.
* **Interaction of Gaps:** Notably, posting `4631822008` (Alignment) flipped to `RELEVANT`, even though Claude’s primary objection there was role type rather than level. Removing the level objection via the explicit-only rule was sufficient to tip its combined evaluation, showing that the two hypothesized gaps are not fully independent in practice.

---

## 6. Limitations & Unresolved Caveats
* **Held-Out Ceiling Effect:** Both prompts scored 10/10 on the held-out set. Because the held-out set contained no research-blended roles with implicit seniority signals (relying instead on clear-cut failures like explicit "Staff+" titles, London/Paris locations, and management roles), it never tested the specific failure mode the revision targeted. Consequently, held-out data cannot confirm or refute whether the change generalizes.
* **Sample Size & Variance:** Each evaluation consisted of only 1 call per posting across 10 postings per set, and sampling runs demonstrated that single calls can exhibit verdict variance (e.g., the 3–1 split observed on posting `4613568008`).
* **Post-Hoc Tuning & Label Limitations:** The revision change was selected and tuned by inspecting the dev set misses, meaning the +3 performance gain on dev may reflect overfitting to those specific 10 examples. Furthermore, my hand labels were not fully blind (see `experiment/LABELING_NOTES.md`) and reflect my narrow reading of "research"; the revision matches my standard better, which is not the same as being objectively more correct.
