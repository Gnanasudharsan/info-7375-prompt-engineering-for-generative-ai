Context
On 2026-03-02 we chose PostgreSQL for the tracking service (implemented in
TRK-112). Since the regional carrier partnership launched in April, scan
volume has risen from about 40,000 to about 900,000 events per day, with
sharp peaks during evening sorting.

What changed
- To hold peak write load, Tom had to move the primary to a much larger
  instance class twice in five weeks. Projected database cost at the
  expected autumn volume is roughly four times what it is today.
- Almost all reads are now "get all events for one shipment ID," which no
  longer needs joins.
- Dashboard joins can move to the analytics warehouse.

Decision
We are reversing the March decision. The tracking service will move from
PostgreSQL to DynamoDB, keyed on shipment ID with scan time as the sort key.

Reasons
- On-demand capacity follows the evening peaks.
- The dominant access pattern is now stable, which was the open concern in
  March.

Risks noted
- Elena loses direct SQL access; the warehouse export must be ready first.
- Migration must not drop events during cutover.

Action items
- Priya: get engineering-manager sign-off on the switch.
- Marcus: open migration ticket.
- Elena: design the DynamoDB-to-warehouse export.
