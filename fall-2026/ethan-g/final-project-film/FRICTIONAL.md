## What surprised me

The real data was messier than I expected. I thought public data would be cleaner than that. I also found that dbt has no built-in not negative test, so I told Claude to write a test. More surprising, pandera, dbt and Great Expectations all passed trips that ended before they started, because no rule asked about them. I had assumed the tools would catch more than I told them to. Claude’s checks looked good but weren’t. Round one caught everything but rejected 220 good trips, and when I asked for a fix, it quietly dropped the negative-fare rule. Each round fixed one problem and created another, which is exactly why AI output has to be tested. Even my own scorer was wrong at first, counting a crashing check as a catch. The tool doing the checking needed checking too.
